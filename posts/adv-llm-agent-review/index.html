A Review of Advanced LLM Agents MOOC

Sunday, June 1, 2025

In this post, I want to talk about the Advanced LLM Agents MOOC by UC Berkeley that I followed and found insightful.

Overview

The courses focus on the current ongoing LLM Agent research in different domains such as security, coding, vulnerability detection, multimodality, formal mathematics etc.

The initial lectures describing on the shift from a using single LLM with a simple prompt to incorporating reasoning, planning, self-reflection, scaling test-time compute really helped catch up on more than year-long developments that are currently ongoing in both academia and industry. This also helped polish my existing understanding of these developments.

Best Lectures

My favourite lectures were Open Training Recipes for Reasoning in Language Models by Hanna Hajishirzi, University of Washington and Multimodal Agents – From Perception to Action by Caiming Xiong, Salesforce AI Research.

I loved the insights and the possibility of approaching training myself from the lecture. The ideas were intuitive and the fact that all the training recipes are open-source in a github repo makes it very approachable.

The lecture by Caiming Xiong introduced OSWorld to me, which was very fascinating since the environment was very similar to what humans operate on day-to-day lives. Few of the challenges and the ways to approach them were also very interesting.

While I was not interested initially in formal maths, the lectures were very intuitive and insightful which made approaching Lean seem very easy. It was interesting to see how different research groups were approaching the same thing in a different manner.

Conclusion

Overall the course gave a very good look into the current expansion of LLM capabilities from a single model to extensive systems including tool use, external knowledge bases as well as test-time improvements via different methods of prompting, planning & reasoning, intermediate step verification etc. across multiple domains to actually make LLM agents useful when deployed in the real world.
